# Ceph and storage services

This page owns storage admission. The closed schemas in
`api/v1alpha1/storage.go` and `api/v1alpha1/storage_native.go` own accepted
fields. [Native service input](../ceph-native-api.md) owns the approved
service vocabulary; [resource contracts](../ceph-resources.md) own the
separate declarative objects. Bootwright fields use camel case; complete
native service documents retain native snake case.
[The compiler boundary](../api.md#compiler-boundary) applies, with its
plan-time refusal point for what this build admits but does not realize.
This page holds the kinds table, the reference namespaces, the CephCluster
field record and the cross-kind invariants; every other kind's field record
lives on the one page its row in the kinds table links, and
[resource contracts](../ceph-resources.md#managed-storage-resource-graph)
state each kind's realization once.

Every Ceph kind names its objects under the [one name rule](../api.md#object-names); no limit depends on a block prefix or on the services a cluster declares.

Every Secret a realized field of this page references is effect material under
[binding](../secrets.md#immutable-binding-and-contexts): the cluster SSH key,
each service binding's secret and the package repository trust bundle are
placed on the hosts or in the cluster, and none is an access credential. The `externalDetails` secret belongs to an arm that
refuses.

Storage children select their owner through `spec.clusterRef`; they do not
repeat the cluster's settings. The reference is required and
is never inferred from directory placement or a sole cluster. A
`placementPolicyRef` is likewise explicit; a sole policy is not selected
automatically. The consumer-specific `CephConsumer.spec.dataFoundation` arm
retains its own domain.

## CephCluster

A `CephCluster`'s settings sit directly under `spec`: the kind is the
engine, so it has no `type` and no `ceph` block (both
[retired](../api.md#retired-fields)). A managed cluster requires `release`,
`cephadm` (with `cephadm.bootstrap.nodeRef`), `topology` and nonempty
`services`; an external one (`management: external`) declares none of the
managed fields, each of which refuses at its path, and kind defaults never
introduce them. Core MON, MGR and
OSD documents are admitted, and so are MDS documents that a CephFilesystem
of the same cluster binds by name. RGW, ingress and NFS documents are admitted under their
[native records](../ceph-native-api.md#rgw-ingress-and-nfs-records).
NVMe-oF gateway documents (`service_type: nvmeof`) are admitted under their
[native record](../ceph-native-api.md#nvme-of-gateway-record) and plan as a
`ceph-service` nvmeof [unit](../ceph.md#nvme-of-subsystems) once the
`nvmeof` image is authored under `spec.images`
([refusal table](../ceph.md#refusal-table)).
Prometheus, Alertmanager, Grafana, node-exporter, ceph-exporter, snmp-gateway,
mgmt-gateway and oauth2-proxy documents are admitted under their
[native records](../ceph-native-api.md#observability-and-management-gateway-records);
each that needs an auxiliary image plans once that image is authored under
`spec.images` and refuses at planning without it;
ceph-exporter runs the Ceph image, and it and the other monitoring services
realize through the
[monitoring unit](../ceph.md#resources-readiness-and-access); mgmt-gateway
and oauth2-proxy realize through the
[management gateway unit](../ceph.md#resources-readiness-and-access). The
core request lists each of them only by its qualified name and eligible hosts,
outside the host request, so that core observation
[tolerates its daemons](../ceph-native-api.md#service-identity-and-references);
planning realizes an rgw service a CephObjectGateway binds, with its TLS
bindings, through the gateway block and an nfs service through its unit; an
ingress plans once its auxiliary images are authored under `spec.images`
and refuses at planning without them
([refusal table](../ceph.md#refusal-table)). A
`spec.serviceBindings` entry is left to the gateway block only for an RGW
service a CephObjectGateway binds and for the ingress that fronts it, and
the credential bindings of a grafana or snmp-gateway service to the monitoring
unit, and the TLS binding of the management gateway to its unit; any other
entry refuses at planning. An RGW document must be bound by a CephObjectGateway of the same
cluster whose `spec.serviceRef` names it; an unbound one refuses at its
`service_id`, because cephadm would deploy it before its realm, zone group and
zone exist. `spec.mgrModules` admits `cephadm` and
`rbd_support`; `nfs` and `prometheus` refuse as derived from a declared NFS or
prometheus service (D159 clause 9), and `smb` refuses as derived from a
declared CephSMBResource cluster; `mirroring` refuses because Bootwright
does not realize CephFS snapshot mirroring; every other module refuses until
its service owner. Retired daemon helpers, topology roles/devices and drivegroup
wrappers refuse with their replacement path, and the retired
`cephadm.workarounds` list refuses with its reason. Machine bindings,
hostnames, labels, sites and locations remain under topology. A declared
`smb`, `iscsi` or `cephfs-mirror` service refuses at its `service_type`: SMB
daemons are generated from a CephSMBResource, and Bootwright does not
realize the iSCSI gateway or CephFS snapshot mirroring (see [service identity](../ceph-native-api.md#service-identity-and-references)).
Unsupported
service families, options with unqualified effects and protected leaves
without a qualified binding record refuse explicitly. There is no arbitrary
provider-command escape hatch.

Other P2 resource additions remain target contracts, not admitted kinds.

IBM packages use the vendor repository selected by `packageRepository`.
The optional `distribution.ibm.packages.source` accepts only `vendor`; subscription
package sources and subscription repository lists are not admitted. Machine
registration remains the Machine installation profile's responsibility.

Future storage export feature families are refused by current pure
admission. Their shared shapes in [field detail](../deferred/ceph-api.md) are
retained only for the later owning slices.

Operators select IBM Ceph release identifiers, package and tool versions,
container tags or digests and related service-version coordinates in input.
Bootwright preserves those selections through native package, container and
Ceph commands, including malformed or nonexistent versions and incompatible combinations,
and reports native failures with their cause and selected coordinate, subject
to secret redaction. It neither checks version existence, vendor support or
mutual compatibility (D157, D163) nor maintains IBM's release table, an approved-version
catalog or per-release profiles. A tested example imposes no version allowlist.
Structural input validation and independent trust, authorization and immutable
artifact requirements still apply; version syntax is interpreted by native
tools rather than a Bootwright semantic-version grammar. The adapter adapts its
mechanics to capabilities the selected artifacts provably have or lack
([version specializations](../architecture.md#implementations-and-version-variation));
that adaptation neither approves nor refuses a selection.

Ordinary apply realizes the core cluster, placement policies, replicated and
erasure-coded pools, RBD images, CephFS filesystems with their subvolume
groups, CephFS subvolumes, native `nfs` services, NFS exports of a CephFS
filesystem, a CephFS subvolume or an object user's buckets, one-way journal
RBD mirroring of a CephReplication, NVMe-oF subsystems, managed
CephConsumers and CephClients of an RBD pool, a CephFS filesystem or a
CephFS subvolume over `verifiedTunnel`; an ingress
plans once its auxiliary images are authored under `spec.images` and
refuses at planning without them.
`distribution` is a [presence union](../api.md#unions) of the arms `oss`,
`redhat` and `ibm`; omission selects `oss: {}`, which effective output shows.
The `oss` arm holds the optional community `mirror` and `checksum`, `redhat`
holds nothing, and `ibm` holds `callHome` and `packages`. Core Ceph realizes
only `ibm`, so planning refuses any other arm, an omitted one included, with
`lifecycle.unsupported` ([refusal table](../ceph.md#refusal-table)).
`release` is the operator's release label;
`packageVersion` selects the Ceph/cephadm RPM roots and
`cephadm.ansible.packageVersion` selects the actual cephadm-ansible RPM.
Optional `nfsGanesha.packageVersion` selects the NFS-Ganesha RPM; NFS
daemons that a declared nfs service deploys run from the frozen Ceph image. These strings are nonempty, bounded to 1024 UTF-8
bytes and control-free; native tools interpret their contents.
`packageRepository` optionally selects an HTTPS RPM repository without
userinfo or fragment, of at most 1798 bytes without trailing slashes, so
every metadata path the host fetches stays within 2048 bytes. Omission uses
`https://public.dhe.ibm.com/ibmdl/export/pub/storage/ceph/9/rhel9/x86_64` as a
source default, not a release catalog. Independent publisher signatures remain
required for its selected RPMs.

`packageRepositoryTrustBundleRef` optionally names a `caBundle` Secret,
a trust anchor, that is the only TLS trust anchor for the authored `packageRepository`:
host package acquisition verifies that repository's metadata and package
downloads against the bundle alone (no host trust store, default path or
environment override), and against the host trust store when the field is
omitted; it never falls back to another repository. It is admitted only with
an authored `packageRepository`, so the source default never takes a
private bundle; a missing Secret or a Secret of another type refuses at
admission. The bundle authenticates the connection only: each selected RPM
still needs its publisher signature.

`packageRepositoryKey` is required when `packageRepository` is
authored and forbidden otherwise. It names the unchanged IBM publisher key
at an HTTPS URL of at most 2048 bytes, without userinfo, query, fragment or
traversal. Its scheme, host (case-insensitive) and effective port must match
the repository; omitted HTTPS ports mean 443. The key may be outside the
repository's path. The repository's trust bundle alone verifies the key
connection when bound, otherwise the host trust store does. Neither acquisition
falls back to a public origin. When both fields are omitted, the default
repository uses IBM's public `RPM-GPG-KEY-IBM-CEPH` under
`https://public.dhe.ibm.com/ibmdl/export/pub/storage/ceph/`.
The frozen core and host requests retain the exact selected key URL. Serving
a key from a mirror never changes the code-pinned IBM fingerprint: a mismatched
key or unsigned RPM refuses before package installation.

The `ibm` arm's `callHome` is required, with no default, and is `enabled` or
`disabled`. `enabled` keeps the provider's Call Home reporting active after
bootstrap; `disabled` leaves it inactive. The selected build decides how an
intent is realized: on a build whose Call Home is opt-in, `disabled` is the
native default and requires the `call_home_agent` module not enabled, and
`enabled` refuses `call-home-unqualified`; on a build whose Call Home is
automatic, `disabled` refuses `call-home-unqualified`. Either refusal comes
before the cluster is claimed but after the ceph-host blocks ran from the
frozen input, so a corrected `callHome` is an input change: `bootwright
destroy` takes back what the apply owns, then `context update` and a fresh
apply carry it (D162).

`image.base` names the repository and `image.version` supplies the
authored tag or SHA-256 digest. The Entitlement registry namespace must contain
the selected repository; credentials are scoped to its authority. A direct
digest is resolved independently of any tag. Native acquisition and platform
inspection freeze the selected immutable artifact for continuation.
The [release qualification](../ceph-coverage.md#release-qualification) owns
those proofs. Validation proves no live qualification; [M2b](../milestones/m2.md#m2b)
owns delivery and operator acceptance.

`images.<name>` authors one auxiliary image. Its keys are cephadm's
`mgr/cephadm/container_image_<name>` setting suffixes: `alertmanager`,
`alloy`, `grafana`, `haproxy`, `keepalived`, `loki`, `nginx`,
`node_exporter`, `nvmeof`, `oauth2_proxy`, `prometheus`, `promtail`, `samba`,
`samba_metrics` and `snmp_gateway`; `base` is not a key, because `image`
owns the Ceph image. Each holds `base` and `version` under the rules of
`image`: an IBM distribution requires both, the base must be beneath the
Entitlement registry namespace, and the version is a tag or a SHA-256 digest,
nonempty, at most 1024 UTF-8 bytes and control-free, which native tools
interpret. An image is required exactly when a declared service or a
CephSMBResource of the cluster needs it; planning refuses a missing one and
one that nothing declared needs
([refusal table](../ceph.md#refusal-table)). Bootwright keeps no default and
no table of values. Resolution freezes each image's linux/amd64 digest with
the Ceph image ([frozen release and trust](../ceph.md#frozen-release-and-trust)).

`cephadm.clusterSSH.transport` accepts `native` or `verifiedTunnel`
(D135). Omission selects `verifiedTunnel`, the only transport a build
realizes; `native` is reserved and `validate` refuses it, and an empty, null or
unknown value refuses. The frozen core request records the effective transport
for the cluster and its children. A verified tunnel never qualifies the direct
native channel, and this field grants no exception to host-key verification.
The three-node example selects `verifiedTunnel` explicitly; its protected
mounts and staged bootstrap remain subject to
[release qualification](../ceph-coverage.md#release-qualification).

Core request v11 refuses continuation of v10 operations, image request v2 and
package request v2 refuse continuation of older operations, and core
evidence v3 and host request v2 refuse continuation of operations an
earlier build made; finish or remove old contexts with their original
applying build. The CephFS CephClient scopes move the core request again
and the optional specializations evidence member moves the host evidence; a
context or incomplete operation an earlier build planned is finished or
destroyed with that build, while a core evidence record without the member
still reads. A host preparation request carries only its cluster's
scope, never the core request's resource action, pool, client or image,
so a resource change never moves it.

The existing native-section config map admits explicit fixed-memory and
pool-default pairs for the minimal core configuration:

```yaml
config:
  global:
    osd_pool_default_size: "2"
    osd_pool_default_min_size: "2"
  osd:
    osd_memory_target_autotune: "false"
    osd_memory_target: "2147483648"
```

Each pair is optional and requires both of its string values together.
The global pair admits only explicit 2/2 defaults. It affects newly created
pools, including provider-created pools, and does not resize an existing pool
or replace its CephPool declaration. It is rendered in the initial global
bootstrap section and verified against the native configuration database.
Omission preserves the provider's pool defaults; there is no silent cluster
size-based change.
The memory target is canonical decimal
bytes, at least 2 GiB and within the native unsigned 64-bit size domain.
Unknown sections, other options, automatic tuning and competing scopes refuse;
there is no arbitrary config pass-through. The pair is frozen with cluster
ownership, assimilated before OSD deployment and verified against the native
configuration database and each running OSD's reported effective values.
Omission or an empty map preserves provider defaults. This best-effort target
is neither a process memory cap nor qualification of a reduced host budget;
provider sizing and measured resource acceptance remain separate.

### Normalization

After [Environment defaults](../api.md#defaults-normalization-and-effective-state)
apply, the cluster normalizer materializes these values; every other authored
value is preserved:

- `management: managed` when omitted; the rest of this list applies to a
  managed cluster only;
- `distribution: {oss: {}}` when omitted, which effective output shows;
- for an `oss` distribution with an exact `X.Y.Z` release and no
  `image.version`, `image.version: vX.Y.Z`, and an `oss.checksum` spelled
  `sha256:` followed by lowercase digits;
- `cephadm.clusterSSH.user: cephadm` and `transport: verifiedTunnel` in an
  authored `cephadm`, and `cephadm.bootstrap.addressRef` from
  `cephadm.addressRef`, else the bootstrap node's Machine
  `access.ssh.addressRef`;
- `networks.publicCIDRs` and `networks.clusterCIDRs` with host bits masked;
- each topology node's `fqdn` as `<node>.<cluster>.<storage domain>` when the
  single Environment declares a storage domain (`domains.storageClusters`,
  else `domains.clusters`, else `domains.base`), and its `site` from its
  Machine's `placement.site`;
- a `topology.stretch` block's `ruleName: stretch-rule`, its tiebreaker
  `site` from the tiebreaker node and, when omitted, its `dataSites` as the
  sorted distinct sites of the nodes the MON service places, other than the
  tiebreaker; and
- `serviceBindings` and their `secrets` in sorted order.

Implicit defaults owned by Ceph or a native renderer, such as role-derived
placement, inferred pool application and monitoring enablement when merely
implicit, remain absent from effective state. Each other kind's normalization
is stated on its field record.

## Complete-graph invariants

In addition to the field-local rules of the schema, desired-state validation
checks:

- management mode, distribution, entitlement, image, package, bootstrap,
  topology, network, FIPS, CephX, and cluster-login declarations agree;
- each `topology.nodes[].machineRef` names a Machine, which is a Ceph node
  because the topology names it ([substrate arm](machines.md#substrate-arm));
- native placement resolves to owned Machines and service counts fit finite
  candidate sets, with consistent site and label bindings;
- OSD selectors are bounded and internally consistent and exclude the
  Machine's root device from explicit data, DB, and WAL paths;
- policies, pools, filesystems, gateways, NFS exports, SMB resources, and
  consumers reference the same cluster under each kind's management-mode constraints and
  use compatible pool roles and protection modes;
- addresses, CIDRs, DNS labels, paths, ports, service IDs, VRRP IDs, config
  ownership, and Secret types do not conflict across the aggregate graph;
- ingress virtual IPs are `address/prefix` inside a declared public CIDR with
  its prefix length, neither its network nor its broadcast address, and
  equal, after parsing, to no Machine address and no other ingress virtual
  IP; a management gateway ingress instead carries a bare address inside a
  declared public CIDR, equal after parsing to the gateway's `virtual_ip`;
- the listener registry resolves every native listener with the release
  defaults before comparing (IBM's `get_port_start`): on every storage host
  the Ceph monitors (3300, 6789), the messenger range 6800-7568
  ([upstream](https://github.com/ceph/ceph/blob/v20.2.1/src/common/options/global.yaml.in#L1221-L1234))
  and, unless a ceph-exporter service is declared, ceph-exporter (9926); on
  the MGR service's hosts the MGR dashboard (8443), cephadm service discovery
  (8765) and, when a prometheus service is declared, the prometheus manager
  module (9283)
  ([upstream](https://github.com/ceph/ceph/blob/v20.2.1/src/pybind/mgr/cephadm/services/monitoring.py#L476));
  on the management gateway's hosts its internal listener (29443)
  ([upstream](https://github.com/ceph/ceph/blob/v20.2.1/src/pybind/mgr/cephadm/services/mgmt_gateway.py#L21));
  the `port` of prometheus (9095), alertmanager (9093), grafana (3000),
  node-exporter (9100), ceph-exporter (9926), snmp-gateway (9464), loki
  (3100), promtail (9080), alloy (its authored `port` only) and the
  management gateway (required, no default), the Alertmanager cluster
  listener (9094) and oauth2-proxy (4180, whatever `https_address` names), as
  IBM's ServiceSpec defaults them; on a loki service's hosts its fixed HTTP
  (3100) and gRPC (8080) listeners and on a promtail service's hosts its fixed
  listener (9080), whatever `port` is authored, because cephadm's loki and
  promtail configuration binds them and uses `port` only for its own port
  check and firewall opening
  ([upstream](https://github.com/ceph/ceph/blob/v20.2.1/src/pybind/mgr/cephadm/templates/services/loki.yml.j2)); an RGW service's `rgw_frontend_port` (80, or 443
  with `ssl`); an NFS service's `port` (2049), `monitoring_port` (9587) and
  its fixed cluster QoS listener (31311); and an ingress service's
  `frontend_port`, `monitor_port` and HAProxy peer port (1024 for an NFS
  backend unless `haproxy_peer_communication_port` names another). Each
  listener holds every eligible host of its service's placement. An ingress
  frontend binds its virtual IP
  ([upstream](https://github.com/ceph/ceph/blob/v20.2.1/src/pybind/mgr/cephadm/services/ingress.py#L206-L211)),
  an NFS `port` with a `virtual_ip` and without the HAProxy protocol binds
  that `virtual_ip`
  ([upstream](https://github.com/ceph/ceph/blob/v20.2.1/src/pybind/mgr/cephadm/services/nfs.py#L100-L113)),
  an RGW service with `only_bind_port_on_networks` binds the host's Machine
  address inside its `networks` when one is declared
  ([upstream](https://github.com/ceph/ceph/blob/v20.2.1/src/pybind/mgr/cephadm/services/cephadmservice.py#L1139-L1150)),
  and so do grafana
  ([upstream](https://github.com/ceph/ceph/blob/v20.2.1/src/pybind/mgr/cephadm/services/monitoring.py#L62-L67))
  and prometheus
  ([upstream](https://github.com/ceph/ceph/blob/v20.2.1/src/pybind/mgr/cephadm/services/monitoring.py#L591-L596)),
  and every other listener binds all addresses. Two listeners on one port of
  one host refuse unless both bind distinct specific addresses; the refusal
  names the host and the other listener;
- two ingress services of any CephCluster whose networks (the virtual
  IP's network and `virtual_interface_networks`) overlap use different
  keepalived virtual router IDs; an omitted `first_virtual_router_id` is 50,
  and each ingress takes one ID because `virtual_ips_list` is refused;
- each declared rgw service is bound by exactly one CephObjectGateway of
  its cluster; realm, zone group and zone are unique per cluster across
  gateways; zone pool roles and the derived-pool host rule hold; and endpoint
  FQDNs are unique across storage node hostnames and gateways
  ([field record](../ceph-resources.md#cephobjectgateway-field-record));
- each object user binds a same-cluster CephObjectGateway, never takes the
  user ID `dashboard`, and has a display name of at most 256 bytes without
  control characters that does not begin with `-`
  ([field record](../ceph-resources.md#cephobjectuser-field-record));
- each object bucket binds a same-cluster CephObjectGateway and a
  same-cluster CephObjectUser of that gateway, its name has 3 to 44
  characters, and its policy, when declared, has a `Version` string and a
  non-empty `Statement`
  ([field record](../ceph-resources.md#cephobjectbucket-field-record));
- each NFS export binds exactly one same-cluster filesystem, subvolume or
  object user and one declared nfs service, its pseudo path is unique among
  the exports of that service, and its native record is closed and serves
  NFSv4 over TCP with `sys` security only; an object user's export has
  `fsal.name: RGW` and path `/`, and its cluster declares exactly one
  CephObjectGateway
  ([field record](../ceph-resources.md#cephnfsexport));
- SMB clusters of one storage cluster place on disjoint hosts, and each
  holds the SMB port (445) and SMB metrics port (9922) on every eligible
  host, and CTDB (4379) when clustered
  ([upstream](https://github.com/ceph/ceph/blob/v20.2.1/src/python-common/ceph/smb/constants.py#L39-L41)),
  binding all addresses, in the listener comparison above;
- SMB public addresses are `address/prefix` inside a declared public CIDR
  with its prefix length, neither its network nor its broadcast address, and
  equal to no Machine address, no ingress virtual IP and no other SMB public
  address;
- an SMB share and an NFS export of one filesystem must bind distinct
  CephSubvolumes, two SMB clusters never share one subvolume, and SMB
  native identities, share names and auth references resolve within the
  cluster ([field record](../ceph-resources.md#cephsmbresource)); and
- an NFS service's mode follows its ingress, and the HAProxy mode that hides
  client addresses refuses
  ([NFS modes](../ceph-native-api.md#rgw-ingress-and-nfs-records));
- every MON host's address, from `cephadm.addressRef` or else the Machine's
  `access.ssh.addressRef`, lies inside one of `networks.publicCIDRs`; and
- a `topology.stretch` block declares a `failureDomain` that is a native
  default CRUSH bucket type above `host` (`chassis`, `rack`, `row`, `pdu`,
  `pod`, `room`, `datacenter`, `zone` or `region`), exactly two `dataSites`,
  each holding a MON and at least two OSD hosts, and a `tiebreaker.node` that
  the MON service places, at its own `tiebreaker.site` distinct from both,
  that is not the bootstrap node and that no other service places; every
  other node sits at a data site; a node `location` names only the failure
  domain, equal to its site, the tiebreaker none, and a cluster without
  stretch none; a CephPlacementPolicy of a stretch cluster and a CephPool
  `placementPolicyRef` naming one refuse, and a pool's protection is `4/2`
  ([stretch topology](../ceph.md#stretch-topology)); and
- on a stretch cluster, each `mgr`, `mds` and `rgw` service's resolved hosts
  reach both data sites and its `count` exceeds its hosts at either site;
  each rgw ingress's hosts lie at one data site, and an `rgw` service has no
  ingress or one per data site, alike in port, TLS and bound Secret; an
  `nvmeof` or `nfs` service, a CephNFSExport and a CephSMBResource refuse
  ([stretch services](../ceph.md#stretch-services-and-front-doors)).

A FIPS cluster admits a provided Machine, whose FIPS mode its host block
observes ([frozen release and trust](../ceph.md#frozen-release-and-trust));
planning admits a provided Machine only as the stretch tiebreaker
([stretch topology](../ceph.md#stretch-topology)). The MON address proof
covers each MON host whose address reference resolves to a declared IP
literal; a DNS or runtime-assigned address is proved by cephadm's own
placement.

These checks qualify desired intent only. Live capacity, device safety,
external-cluster identity and native-output interpretation remain gates of
the owning lifecycle slices. They do not authorize version-combination
screening: native tools determine version availability and compatibility.
