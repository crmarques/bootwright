# M1 — Hardening

Security, code and architecture improvement, and bug fixes. M1 holds the
pre-OpenShift readiness program the owner started on 2026-09-26, the 2026-09
audit follow-ups, the owner's full bar of 2026-10-05, and the safety, recovery
and operability items that serve no single product milestone. The
[product scope](../project.md#product-scope) names no platform for it; it
serves the [design priorities](../project.md#design-priorities-and-non-goals)
of least privilege and fail-closed effects.

**Requires:** none. **Exit tier:** in-tree, plus the real-host run of
[B49](delivered.md#x38--the-real-host-run-of-2026-10-05) and the extended
lab-rhel run D59 sets on a clean build of X44's landing. **Open decisions:**
none; D117 and D118 settled the last two on 2026-10-06. **Scope:** the owner's full
bar (D56, which amends D48): well-defined specs, architecture and code that
follow best practices, domain-constrained APIs with input validation, clear
CLI output, and a CLI ready for every controller function and for RHEL
machines on libvirt and sushy-tools. It holds what the 2026-10-05 audit found
in M1's journeys, the parked items D56 attached and the items B278 to B302 and
B336 to B338 it added. Since D116, which amends D56 and D48, a safety or
defect follow-up that a slice from X39 on finds in M1's journeys joins M1 on
X46, the sweep slice before the closing run, or on an earlier planned slice
whose lane already owns its files, as ten of X39's did; any other follow-up
stays parked in the [backlog](backlog.md) under D48. The real-hardware test
follows M1 (D60).

## Item detail

### B19

One implementation of
[the adapter result protocol](../architecture.md#the-adapter-result-protocol),
and one Ansible runner: a shared plugin base with per-capability evidence
functions, and one Go decoder over the protocol table X11 delivered. Its hang
clause landed as S14 in X14. Ten per-capability action plugins each carry
their own copy of the protocol's phase dispatch, group emission and
postcondition rule, and each is paired with a documentation-only module; only
the record emission and its acknowledgement are shared. Beside them,
`controller/ansiblelocal` is a second runner with a second protocol reader with
a different record bound and a `prepared` phase the lifecycle runner does not
have, so `setup` and the controller stage cross a different boundary from
every other block. The lifecycle decoder also admits case-variant duplicate
members, null or non-object evidence and non-canonical spacing, which the
controller decoder refuses, so a non-conforming record can end an attempt
`failed` where it must end `unknown`. The 2026-10-05 audit found that, and
found this item's two race clauses settled: X32 fixed the refusal that raced
the adapter's exit, and the controller's release at its next acknowledgement
is specified behavior. D99 settles the rest: progress emission tasks stay in
roles, postcondition decisions stay in Go (D11), and the decoder's strictness
stays in this item. Collapsing both changes what every protocol plugin does
and what fails a run, and no in-tree gate proves an adapter's real host
behavior. **Exit evidence:** one shared protocol implementation with its own
unit tests; a decoder that refuses case-variant duplicate members, null or
non-object evidence and non-canonical spacing; one runner whose request,
phases and failure semantics both consumers share; per-capability evidence
proved by its own tests; the collection sanity, lint and units suites over the
merged plugins; a real-host apply and destroy over the one runner, in D59's
owner-accepted closing run; and `make quick`, `make docs-check`, `make check`,
`make race` and an independent review of the diff.

Since X29 the bare-metal identity rule lives both in the physical protocol action and in Go's evidence decoder, like the other per-capability copies this item tracks.

Since X31 the lifecycle protocol decoder also accepts a `refused` record limited to the reasons a run request lists; the merged decoder carries both runners' `refused` rules.

### B270

10-second stop timeout, and its unit template sets no stop timeout or success
status, so every stop or removal waits ten seconds, ends with SIGKILL (status
137) and leaves the unit `failed` in systemd. The emulated BMC (sushy-tools)
unit did the same until X43: the emulator runs as process 1 with no SIGTERM
handler, and
[X43](delivered.md#x43--libvirt-roles-managed-os-installation-network-composition-and-controller-automation)

2026-10-03 removing lab-rhel's `bootwright-lab-rhel-proxy-lab-proxy` and
`bootwright-lab-rhel-bmc-rhel-01` units while preparing B49's run; the destroy
itself completed.

Seen again on 2026-10-05 in B49's run: each removal, and the host restart, stopped both units with status 137 and left them `failed`, and after the last removal both stayed `failed` until the owner's `systemctl reset-failed` at 11:23 UTC.

X24 gives Squid a short shutdown lifetime in the merged role ([B20](#b20)).
**Exit evidence:** a proxy unit template whose stop ends cleanly, with its
template golden, and, on a host after both X43 and X24, no unit left failed
after a destroy; and `make quick`, the collection's units and lint suites,
`make docs-check`, `make check`, `make race` and an independent review of the
diff.
